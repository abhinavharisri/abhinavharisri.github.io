How a UPI payment actually moves

Five parties, a few seconds, and why the app you tap is only the front door.

3 min read

When I tap Pay in GPay, it feels like the app is moving my money. It isn't. The app is the only part I can see, but it does the smallest part of the job. Everything important happens between NPCI and two banks.

The cast

PSP app
GPay, PhonePe, Paytm. The interface. It collects what I type and forwards the request.
NPCI
The traffic controller. Every request goes through its APIs, which route money between banks.
Issuer bank
Where the money leaves. It checks my PIN and debits my account.
Beneficiary bank
Where the money lands. It credits the receiver.
VPA
The address, like name@bank. Nobody needs an account number or IFSC code.
UPI PIN
The authentication. Proof that it's really me.

Unlike a wallet, nothing has to be loaded in first. UPI pulls straight from the bank account, in real time.

NPCI is the traffic controller, banks hold the money, and the app is just the window you look through.

The flow, step by step

  1. The app collectsReceiver's UPI ID or number, the amount, and my PIN. Then it forwards the request.
  2. NPCI routesIt works out which banks are involved and passes the request along.
  3. Issuer bank debitsMy bank authorises the payment and takes the money out.
  4. Beneficiary bank creditsThe receiver's bank puts the money in.
  5. Confirmation travels backOnly when the banks confirm through NPCI does my app show success.

That last step is the interesting one. If a bank is slow to confirm, the app has nothing to show except "Processing". That gap gets its own note.

Where cards fit in

Card payments run on different pipes, with a few more pieces in the middle:

  • Payment gateway (Razorpay, Stripe, PayPal) connects the merchant to the networks and banks, and moves payment data securely.
  • Card network (Visa, Mastercard) carries the request to the bank that issued the card, which approves or declines it.
  • Encryption keeps card details unreadable while they travel.
  • Tokenisation replaces the card number with a token, so the merchant can charge you again without ever storing your real card.
