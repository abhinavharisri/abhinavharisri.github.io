How free UPI apps make money

Sending money costs users nothing. So who pays for all of it?

3 min read

Card payments have a simple answer: the merchant pays a small cut of every transaction, called the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR). That fee funds the whole system.

UPI is different. In India, MDR on UPI is set to zero, and the regulator keeps it there. So the payment itself, the thing the app is famous for, earns it almost nothing. The money has to come from around the payment instead.

Where it actually comes from

Bill pay & recharges
Electricity, mobile recharges, FASTag. Commissions and partnerships on each one. This is why every app keeps adding them.
Merchant services
Tools for shops, like payment soundboxes, that the merchant pays for even though the payments are free.
Financial services
Loans, credit cards, insurance, buy-now-pay-later. Where the real margin is.

The long game is lending

If I had to bet on one stream long term, it's financial services. A payments app sees how people actually spend, every day. That doesn't mean selling raw data. It means using it for better risk assessment, better targeting, and better product decisions: who to offer credit to, how much, and when. That's what turns free payments into a profitable business.

Which is why trust comes first

None of this works unless people trust the app with their money first. Nobody takes a loan from an app whose payments keep failing. The free, reliable payment is what earns the right to sell everything else.

Trust comes before monetisation in fintech. Nobody hands their money to an app they don't trust.
