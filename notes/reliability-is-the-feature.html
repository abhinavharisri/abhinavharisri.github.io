Reliability is the feature

Why a payments app should fix its success rate before shipping anything new, and how I'd defend that to a CEO.

5 min read

Almost everyone opens a payments app for one reason: to pay. If that works fast and every time, they come back. If it's slow or throws errors, they leave, and no new feature will bring them back.

The numbers that matter

Success rate
The share of payments that go through. The core job. When it drops, usage drops, and eventually people stop opening the app at all.
DAU / MAU
How many monthly users open the app every day. Payments are daily habits, like the 8 AM milk run or the afternoon chai. A high ratio means the app has become the default.
Revenue per user
Decides how long the app survives. And it only grows if people keep using it.

The decision

Users trust a payments app only when transactions work consistently. So the team should prioritise successful payment % over new features.

Who benefits: users, who get payments they can rely on, and the business, which gets more engagement, a smaller support load, and budget freed up for features later.

What it costs: new features ship more slowly, some users will want something new, and the team has to put real work into unglamorous fixes.

Defending it under pressure

Imagine the CEO says: “Our competitors are launching new features every month. Why are we slowing down?”

My answer: look at their success rates and active users, not their launch calendar. If we ship features without fixing the basics, there's nothing to tell us apart from them.

Feature velocity matters, but in payments, reliability drives trust and retention. Fix it first, and every feature we ship after builds on a stable foundation instead of hiding the friction.

The data I'd bring: DAU and MAU over time, with competitors' feature launches marked on the timeline, to show what happens when features ship on top of unresolved problems.

The compromise I'd offer: split engineering capacity so we stay visible in the market without losing focus.

70% reliability30% one visible feature a quarter

The spin-wheel test

Marketing pitches a "Daily Reward Spin Wheel" on the home screen. Spin once a day for small cashback. It boosts engagement, competitors are gamifying, DAU goes up.

My answer: not right now. If payments are still unreliable, a spin wheel is decoration on a cracked foundation. Once reliability is solid, it can make sense in short, timed bursts, during the IPL, a big football match, or the festive season, when the whole country is already excited about something.

Payments are the core job. If reliability isn't strong, engagement features won't turn into long-term value.
