Fixing the pending screen

Reducing user anxiety during pending UPI transactions: the problem, two options, and why I'd ship the quieter one first.

5 min read

Context
UPI users regularly hit pending transactions, which lead to panic, mistrust and support tickets.
Problem
Money is debited, confirmation is delayed, and the user is left alone with the word "Pending".
Insight
The stress doesn't come from waiting. It comes from fearing the money is lost, and not knowing what happens next.

Two options on the table

Feature AA retry buttonLet users try the payment again when it fails or hangs.
Feature B · ship firstA better pending screenClear message, a timeline, and next steps, instead of one word.

Why B goes first

B is low-to-medium effort with high impact on trust. But the bigger reason is what A does when things go wrong.

If the sender's bank server is down and people keep pressing retry without knowing what's happening, every tap is another request. That piles load onto our own servers and slows the app down for everyone else too. A retry button on a pending screen can turn one bank's bad afternoon into an outage.

The best fix for a scary state isn't a button to escape it. It's telling people what's going on.

What the new screen does

  • Replaces "Pending" with a sentence that says what's happening, in plain language.
  • Shows a timeline: when the money will arrive, or when it will come back if it doesn't.
  • After a set time (I'd say 15 minutes), switches to next steps and a direct way to contact support.
  • Adds delivery ticks to the payment in the receiver's history, like WhatsApp's sent and delivered ticks, so the sender can see the money actually landed.

How I'd measure it

  1. Fewer support ticketsThe first number to move, and the easiest to see.
  2. Fewer duplicate retriesPeople who understand what's happening stop hammering the button.
  3. Lower uninstall rateOne bad pending experience is a common reason people leave a payments app.
  4. Time in the app after a paymentCalm users stick around. Panicked users close it.

Tradeoffs

It isn't free. Accurate timelines and delivery ticks need more backend coordination to track real status, and the copy and design need real care, because a wrong promise is worse than no promise. Delaying the retry button also carries a risk: some users will want it. If support and instructions are good, that risk stays small.

What I took from it

The hardest part of choosing a feature is knowing whether it will actually matter: is it needed, does it change anything for the user, and what does it cost the team? Saying no to a feature is hard because you never know what you're giving up. But the risk has to be one the business can survive.

Small, well-targeted changes can have a large impact on user trust.
