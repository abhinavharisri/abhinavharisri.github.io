Note 02 · Payments

P2P builds the habit. P2M pays the bills.

Two kinds of UPI payment, two very different jobs for the business.

3 min read

Every UPI payment is one of two kinds. They use the same rails, but they do completely different things for the company behind the app.

P2P · Peer to peerPerson to personSend to a UPI ID or phone number. No IFSC code. Rent, splitting dinner, paying back a friend.
P2M · Peer to merchantCustomer to businessScan a shop's QR or pay at checkout. Tagged as a merchant payment, and it can carry an order ID or bill details.

One small detail I like in P2M: the app shows the merchant's registered name before you pay. It's a quiet fraud check. You can see the money is going to the shop, not to someone pretending to be it.

P2P grows users

P2P is about habit. You send money to friends and family, they send it back, and before long it's simply how you move money. It spreads through people who already trust each other, which is why it's the easy one to scale.

P2M grows the business

P2M is commerce. Shops take payments by QR, checkouts get faster, queues get shorter, and the business sells more. That's where scale and revenue live. It's also harder: every merchant has to be won over one at a time, and they need a reason to switch.

P2P drives habit and trust. P2M drives revenue and scale.

Why apps start with P2P

Almost every payments app starts with P2P. People try it with friends, learn how it works, start trusting it. Only then does the app go after merchants and look for ways to make money.

GPay is the clearest example I've used. It started as a way to send money to people I know. Now it's how I pay at shops too.
